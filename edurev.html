Attendance beyond the roll call

Recognised activity outside the classroom can support attendance relaxation and duty leaves.

  • Hackathons, technical competitions and certified workshops.
  • Club, society and community-service responsibilities.
  • Approved internships, field work and industry visits.
Reality check: this is not a free pass. Submit the activity through EDU-Revolution and get it approved before claiming relaxation. Scheduled classes still need a valid reason.
50%

Half your learning happens by doing

Hands-on engagement, technical competitions, hackathons and certifications replace passive revision.

  • Build a project that solves a real problem, not a tutorial clone.
  • Use NPTEL, SWAYAM or MOOC courses to fill a specific skill gap.
  • Learn from a paid internship, freelance brief or startup sprint.
Where to start: UMS → LMS → EDU-Revolution lists expert-defined options for your courses. You can also use the “Any Other” custom application route.
50%

Outcomes that speak for themselves

Grading can reflect real-world outcomes — shipped projects, client work, research and certifications.

  • Grade upgradation in core or non-core courses.
  • Course exemption through Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL).
  • Value addition on your academic transcript.
Proof matters: a live URL, GitHub repository, client testimonial or verified certificate is stronger than a description. Upload evidence at the draft stage.
The four pillars

What EDU-Revolution is built on

Four connected promises: learn by doing, earn while learning, choose a career path, and help decide how the university works.

Pillar 01

Gamified Learning with AI Focus

Learning outside the classroom through hands-on engagement, technical competitions, hackathons and certifications.

See the benefits
  • Course exemption
  • Grade upgradation — core and non-core
  • Attendance relaxation and duty leaves
  • Value addition in your academic transcript
Pillar 02

Student-Centric Revenue Generation

Earn income during your studies and target generating at least 50% of your semester fee through approved work.

Revenue avenues
  • Industry and field consultancy projects
  • Freelancing activities
  • Paid internships
  • Start-ups and entrepreneurial ventures
  • University support via “Earn While You Learn” and publication or patent incentives

Submit a proposal covering revenue model, market feasibility, execution strategy and financial projections.

Pillar 03

Career Pathways

“Your journey, your choice: build, serve, or study for tomorrow.” Align your curriculum with where you actually want to go.

Explore the pathways
  • Corporate jobs — product and service companies
  • Higher studies and study abroad
  • Government jobs and public services
  • Entrepreneurship and research
Pillar 04

Students in Decision Making

At least 20% of institutional ideas should originate directly from students across key committees.

Meet the committees
  • Student Academic Advisory Committee — curriculum, teaching and evaluation methods.
  • Student Research and Innovation Committee — funding and real-world problems.
  • Student Career and Campus Connect Committee — internships, companies and social media.
  • Student Start-up Committee — entrepreneurial focus areas.
Category explorer

Nine ways to earn recognition

Tap a category to see what it covers, what proof to upload, and a practical first step.

9 categories
Category 01

Revenue Generation

Earn income through open projects, freelancing and start-up work. The framework encourages students to target at least 50% of their semester fee through legitimate, approved avenues.

  • Industry or field consultancy
  • Freelance briefs and paid internships
  • Start-up revenue and product sales
Proposal checklist: revenue model, market feasibility, execution strategy and financial projections. First step: pick one paid brief and document the scope, deliverable and payment proof.
Category 02

Projects & Hackathons

Standardised projects and hackathon builds give you hands-on experience and a portfolio of real-world problem solving.

  • A working demo, live URL or GitHub repository
  • Problem statement, approach and impact
  • Team details and your specific contribution
First step: register a 24–48 hour build around a campus problem. A small, finished project beats a large, unfinished idea.
Category 03

Internship Opportunities

Practical work experience taken outside regular academic requirements — national or international. It can complement attendance, learning and career outcomes.

  • Offer letter and completion certificate
  • Supervisor contact and duration
  • Work summary, deliverables and skills gained
First step: speak to your mentor before accepting an internship that overlaps with classes. Get the academic fit approved early.
Category 04

NPTEL / SWAYAM / MOOCs

Online courses from top institutions offer quality learning that can map back to your programme outcomes.

  • Verified certificate and assessment score
  • Course syllabus and duration
  • A short note connecting the course to a specific subject
First step: choose a course that fills a gap in your current semester, not a random certificate. Check the expert-defined options in UMS first.
Category 05

Grade Upgradation (Core / Non-Core)

A provision to improve previously earned grades through approved academic activities. If you re-register for a backlog or improvement, the “better grade shall prevail” rule applies.

  • Map the activity to the course outcomes
  • Attach the project, exam or certification proof
  • Track the category in-charge approval
First step: ask the course faculty which outcome your activity demonstrates. Approval is stronger when the link is explicit.
Category 06

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL)

Course exemptions based on knowledge you already have from outside formal classrooms — work, certifications or self-directed learning.

  • Certifications, portfolio and work samples
  • A skills-to-syllabus mapping
  • Assessment or interview if requested
First step: download the course syllabus and highlight the outcomes you can already evidence. Apply before the semester's add/drop window closes.
Category 07

Social Media Presence

Enhance academic visibility and professional networking by sharing what you learn and build — responsibly.

  • Profile or channel links
  • Analytics or reach snapshot
  • A content plan tied to your discipline
First step: publish three posts about a project you completed. Quality and consistency matter more than follower count.
Category 08

Community Service

Meaningful activities addressing social issues such as education, health and welfare — through NSS, NCC, Red Cross or your own initiative.

  • Activity report with dates and hours
  • Photographs and organiser confirmation
  • Measurable impact on the community
First step: join one sustained drive rather than five one-day events. Depth and reflection are easier to evidence.
Category 09

Co-curricular / Extra-curricular

Activities that build professional skills through technical clubs, arts, culture and sports.

  • Club membership or leadership proof
  • Event participation and achievement certificates
  • A reflection on the skills you developed
First step: register for one club during induction, then take a visible role in one event. Your contribution is the evidence.
Step-by-step portal guide

Apply through UMS → LMS

Seven steps from the dashboard to a tracked nomination. Open each step for the exact path and a practical tip.

1Access the portal

Log in to UMS and open UMS → LMS → EDU-Revolution: Be the Change. You can also use the dashboard shortcut “Apply for EDU-Revolution”.

Bookmark the page. You will return here to track approvals.

2Choose a category

Select from Revenue Generation, Projects & Hackathons, Internships, MOOCs, RPL, Grade Upgradation, Social Media, Community Service or Co-curricular activities.

Pick the category that matches your strongest proof, not the one that sounds most impressive.

3Read and accept the undertaking

Review the authenticity undertaking carefully, then click Accept & Continue.

False claims can invalidate a nomination and affect future applications.

4Review expert-defined options

Explore faculty-approved options for specific courses — for example, a Data Structures project mapped to CAP282 — or switch to the “Any Other” custom application tab.

If an expert option fits, use it: the outcome mapping is already defined.

5Fill details and upload proof

Provide the project title, web or GitHub URL, supporting documents and a short description of your contribution. Save a draft if you need more time.

Use descriptive file names and keep each proof easy to verify.

6Review the Edu Rev Cart and submit

Open the cart, review every item, edit or delete drafts, then click Final Submit.

A submitted nomination cannot be edited freely — check dates and links before you submit.

7Track your nomination

Use the Track Nominations tab to follow statuses such as Standing Committee Approval Pending and Category Incharge Approval.

If a nomination is returned, fix the specific evidence requested and resubmit quickly.
